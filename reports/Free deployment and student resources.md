# Cloudflare carries PSYCH at zero cost, commercially

PSYCH can run for $0 on one Cloudflare account, with no card and no non-commercial clause. **Cloudflare Workers with static assets** serves the pre-built Next.js export, **one Worker** runs the API, **D1** holds the small table, **Workers AI** answers questions, and **Turnstile** guards the inputs. This is the only free platform checked that combines unlimited free static-asset requests, a TypeScript runtime, a free SQL database, no sleep, and self-serve terms with no ban on commercial use. Everything else falls short on at least one requirement that matters. **Vercel Hobby** is personal and non-commercial. **GitHub Pages** and **Azure Static Web Apps Free** are framed for personal or non-business sites. **Netlify Free** caps the whole account at 300 credits a month, about 15 GB of bandwidth, which heavy 3D assets use up in roughly 150 visits. **Render** sleeps and deletes free Postgres after 30 days, and **Deno Deploy** shuts down around April 2027. Student programmes help with tools and a free first-year domain, but they do not provide commercial hosting. **Azure for Students** is limited to full-time students and to education or non-commercial use. RMIT's **IT Acceptable Use Standard** bans commercial use of RMIT IT assets. **Val**, RMIT's AI chatbot, has no API. The free stack also imposes hard build rules. Every static file must be **25 MiB or smaller**. The Worker gets **10 ms of CPU** per request. The free WAF allows **one** rate-limit rule. **GitHub Models is retired**. **Gemini's free tier** trains on prompts and cannot serve EEA, UK or Swiss users. **Email Routing needs a domain on Cloudflare DNS**, so client-inquiry email does not work on the free `workers.dev` address alone. The proposed stack decision holds. These findings settle two of its open risks and correct two of its assumptions: the domain plan and the inquiry email path.

## Cloudflare is the only free host that fits all five needs

PSYCH needs five things from a host: CDN delivery of 5–30 MB binaries, a small TypeScript API, a tiny SQL table, no cold-start sleep, and terms that allow a site taking paid freelance inquiries. Cloudflare's static-asset billing page states that **"Requests to static assets are free and unlimited"**. Only requests that run the Worker script are billed against the Workers quota, and a negative route pattern serves assets without invoking the Worker ([CF static assets billing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/static-assets/billing-and-limitations/)). The Free plan allows 100,000 Worker requests a day, 10 ms CPU per request, 128 MB memory, 50 subrequests, 5 Cron Triggers, 20,000 asset files per version and **25 MiB per asset file** ([CF Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)). D1 Free adds 5 million rows read and 100,000 rows written per day, plus 5 GB of storage ([D1 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/pricing/)). The Self-Serve Subscription Agreement has **no non-commercial restriction**. It does bar processing credit-card data on a Free property and placing an "undue burden" on the network, and it lets Cloudflare end Free Services at its discretion ([Cloudflare Terms](https://www.cloudflare.com/terms/)). The Developer Platform terms set no commercial limit on free Workers and no large-file rule ([CF Developer Platform terms](https://www.cloudflare.com/service-specific-terms-developer-platform/)).

The competition fails on bandwidth, terms or longevity. A visitor who loads 100 MB of 3D assets turns 1,000 visits into about 100 GB. That uses the whole monthly allowance on Vercel, GitHub Pages and Azure, and goes well past Netlify's (this is arithmetic on the cited caps, not a measurement).

One wrinkle in Cloudflare's terms needs a note. The Application Services (CDN) terms let Cloudflare limit CDN use for "a disproportionate percentage of pictures, audio files, or other large files" served without the right paid service. They exempt only Enterprise by name and point to the Developer Platform as the proper home ([CF Application Services terms](https://www.cloudflare.com/service-specific-terms-application-services/)). Workers static assets are a Developer Platform product, and those terms contain no such clause. The risk is low, but it is an interpretation, and it is the main reason to have a fallback ready.

## Every backend need has a $0 answer inside one account

The same Cloudflare account covers every backend need except a domain. Keeping it to one vendor keeps one set of credentials, one bill that cannot happen, and one terms document. **Workers AI** gives "10,000 Neurons per day at no charge" on Workers Free, and requests over the limit "fail with an error" rather than bill ([Workers AI pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/pricing/)). That cap is exactly the "overspending impossible" property the design wants. Cloudflare's Developer Platform terms also settle the data question: **"Cloudflare does not use any Customer Content to train generative AI tools"**, and inputs and outputs count as Customer Content ([CF Developer Platform terms](https://www.cloudflare.com/service-specific-terms-developer-platform/)). The earlier research estimated about **500 answers a day** on a 3B model at roughly 2k input and 300 output tokens each, a calculation rather than a measurement ([Workers AI pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/pricing/)).

The KV row deserves attention because it is an easy trap. A per-IP counter written on every chat request would hit KV's 1,000 daily writes after a few hundred conversations. D1's 100,000 writes a day absorbs the same pattern with plenty of room. Turnstile, plus a D1 counter, plus a hard global daily cap set below the Neuron budget, stops a scripted attacker from draining the AI allowance by mid-morning UTC. Note that 00:00 UTC is 10 or 11 am in Melbourne, so the quota resets mid-morning local time.

## The recommended setup, and the fallback if Cloudflare fails

### Recommended: one Cloudflare account, routed so only `/api/*` costs anything

The recommended setup is the stack doc's design with the gaps filled. Next.js `output: 'export'` builds to static files deployed as **Workers static assets**. One **Hono** Worker handles `/api/*` only, with no `run_worker_first` on asset paths. Asset traffic is then unmetered, and the 100,000-request daily quota serves only API calls ([CF static assets billing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/static-assets/billing-and-limitations/)). The Worker binds **Workers AI** for The Order and **D1** for unanswered questions, rate-limit counters and, later, inquiries. A Cron Trigger deletes rows older than 30 days, using one of the 5 free triggers. **Turnstile** fronts the chat and the inquiry form. **Web Analytics** counts visits. **GitHub Actions** on a public repo runs CI and deploys with Wrangler. Raw captures and source splats stay out of git, as the stack doc already requires. GitHub OAuth for the admin page runs in the same Worker. Cookie signing with WebCrypto and a few GitHub API subrequests fit the 10 ms CPU and 50-subrequest limits, since network waits do not count as CPU (an inference from the limits page). **Groq Free**, with Zero Data Retention switched on, stays a dormant second provider behind a feature flag. Total cost is $0 until the domain renews.

### Fallback: same code, different host, smaller assets

The most likely failures are a Cloudflare policy change, enforcement of the large-file CDN clause, or splats that cannot be cut under 25 MiB. Each has a staged answer. The first stage stays on Cloudflare and moves only the oversized binaries to **R2**, whose free limits were not checked and must be confirmed first. The second stage swaps Workers static assets for **Cloudflare Pages + Pages Functions + D1**. Quotas are the same, and Functions count against the same Workers request quota ([CF Pages limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/pages/platform/limits/)). The third stage, if PSYCH must leave Cloudflare entirely, moves to **Netlify Free** for static files, Functions and free form submissions. It would use **Turso** for SQL and **Groq** for inference ([Netlify credits](https://docs.netlify.com/manage/accounts-and-billing/billing/billing-for-credit-based-plans/credit-based-pricing-plans/); [Turso](https://turso.tech/pricing)). That third stage only works if the 3D payload shrinks sharply. At 20 credits per GB and 15 credits per production deploy, the 300-credit cap leaves about 15 GB a month before deploys. The site then pauses rather than bills. Because the frontend is a plain static export and the Worker is one small Hono app, the code moves almost unchanged. The real portability cost lies in the bindings (`env.AI`, `env.DB`), so the Worker should keep them behind a thin interface.

### Domain strategy: free subdomain first, Pack domain when inquiries open

The domain choice decides whether inquiry email works. The free **`*.workers.dev`** address costs nothing and lasts as long as the account. Cloudflare Email Routing and the free `send_email` path to a verified destination need a domain whose DNS Cloudflare hosts ([CF Email Service](https://developers.cloudflare.com/email-service/platform/pricing/); [send email from Workers](https://developers.cloudflare.com/email-routing/email-workers/send-email-workers/)). The docs imply this rather than state it, so it is marked unverified below. **is-a.dev** suits a developer portfolio, since root subdomains "must relate to software development". But it **denies NS records to new users**, so the name cannot be delegated to Cloudflare DNS, and Email Routing on it is ruled out ([is-a.dev FAQ](https://docs.is-a.dev/faq/)). js.org requires JavaScript-ecosystem content. eu.org is meant for people who cannot afford registry fees, which does not fit a business site.

The best $0 path is to claim one **Student Pack domain** shortly before inquiries launch, not now, because the free year starts when it is claimed. The options are Namecheap `.me` (1 year), Name.com (one of 25+ TLDs, term unstated) and get.tech `.tech` (1 year) ([GitHub Student Pack](https://education.github.com/pack)). Point its nameservers at Cloudflare. That one domain then serves the site, hosts Email Routing, and carries Turnstile hostnames. The catch is **year two**. Every Pack domain renews at the registrar's normal price, and premium-style TLDs such as `.tech` or `.app` can renew far above their first-year price. Renewal prices were not verified. Choose the TLD by its renewal price, not its novelty, and register it to a personal email, not the RMIT address. If the owner will not pay renewal, the site can fall back to `workers.dev` with no code change. Inquiries would then go to D1 and the admin page instead of email.

## What the findings confirm and change in the stack decision

The stack decision at `docs/architecture/2026-11-24-tech-stack.md` stands, and three of its listed risks are now closed. Its first risk was **commercial use on Cloudflare's free plan**. The Self-Serve terms contain no non-commercial clause, only the card-data and undue-burden limits ([Cloudflare Terms](https://www.cloudflare.com/terms/)). Its second risk was **static assets counting against the 100k Worker quota**. Asset requests are "free and unlimited", provided only `/api/*` invokes the Worker ([billing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/static-assets/billing-and-limitations/)). Its fourth risk was **Workers AI data use**. Cloudflare does not train on customer content ([Developer Platform terms](https://www.cloudflare.com/service-specific-terms-developer-platform/)). The rejection of Vercel, Supabase and a vector database still holds. Turnstile's free tier is confirmed as unlimited. GitHub Models' retirement does not affect the doc, which never relied on it. Groq stays the right dormant backup.

Four lines need changing. **The domain line is wrong for inquiries.** The doc pairs `workers.dev` with "Cloudflare Email Routing to forward to you", but Email Routing needs a Cloudflare-hosted domain. The fix is to claim a Student Pack domain at inquiry launch (free for year 1, paid renewal after that), or to keep `workers.dev` and read inquiries in the admin page. **The rate-limiting line overstates what is free.** The doc lists a "rate-limit binding" as built in, but its free availability was not confirmed. The confirmed free tools are one WAF rule and D1 counters, and KV's 1,000 daily writes rule it out for counters. **A 25 MiB per-file rule must join the 3D asset pipeline** as a CI check, alongside the existing desktop and mobile splat budgets. **The Worker script-size risk** cited "3 MB in one version". The current limits page lists a Worker size of 64 MiB ([CF Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)). It is unclear whether that figure applies to the Free plan, so the stack doc's "measure in CI" check stays. The doc should also add two items: a CI note that Actions stay free only on a public repo, and a register entry for the CDN large-file clause with R2 or Pages as the mitigation.

## Conclusion

The $0 constraint and the commercial constraint pull in the same direction. Every platform that is generous with bandwidth but calls its free plan "personal" drops out once PSYCH takes a client inquiry. What remains is the one platform whose free tier is designed as a funnel to paid compute rather than a hobby sandbox. That makes Cloudflare's free plan the most defensible host for a freelance portfolio, not merely the cheapest. Two consequences follow for the build. First, the 3D pipeline must treat 25 MiB as a ceiling per file, which favours splitting splats by hour and level of detail, a split the lazy per-hour loading already suggests. Second, the first real cost PSYCH will face is not hosting but a domain renewal in year two, and the only feature that depends on it is inquiry email.

The student angle matters less than expected, and mostly as a list of things to avoid. RMIT's resources and Azure for Students are for learning, so they belong outside the production path. The Student Pack's lasting value is one free first-year domain, claimed when inquiries open, plus tooling. Keep every production account off the RMIT email, and graduation will not break anything.
