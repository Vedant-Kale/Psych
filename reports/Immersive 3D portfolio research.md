# Ten sites already prove PSYCH's parts

No existing site does what PSYCH plans to do as a whole. The research found no portfolio with a dusk-to-dawn journey, no portfolio with a first-person to third-person camera shift, and no portfolio that shows its owner as a realistic capture. But every individual feature has a best-in-class precedent, and most of those precedents are well documented. **Bruno Simon's folio-2025** is the strongest overall reference: MIT-licensed, with a full case study. **Giulio Collesei's cinematic portfolio** is the closest concept, with an owner avatar, film-scene narrative and moody lighting. **Joseph Santamaria's single-take scroll world** is the closest match to a lighting journey driven by scroll. **Tomasz Szmajda's hand-drawn sketchbook site** has the closest "ink fills with paint" effect. **Bradley Matera's ProjectHub** is the best-engineered free-tier AI portfolio assistant. **Maggie Appleton's garden** is the model for the Mind Palace's content structure. Across these sites, the techniques agree. Bake lighting instead of computing it live. Put one camera on an authored path, render only the scene in view, and set quality by device tier. Answer AI facts deterministically and validate citations in code. Keep content as Zod-validated MDX that builds into a JSON graph. For the later tech-stack decisions, these findings point to: no database at launch; Cloudflare Workers AI as the most credible $0 model host; WebGL as the safe default renderer, with WebGPU as an option; Spark for a Gaussian-splat likeness captured with free Scaniverse. These are inputs only. Several of them rest on vendor targets or unverified free-tier terms, not on measurements.

**1. Bruno Simon** remains the reference that other developers cite. The 2019 version won Awwwards SOTD with a Creativity score of 8.95 and a Developer Award, though Accessibility scored only 7.00 ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/bruno-simon-portfolio)). The 2025 rebuild keeps the drive-a-car concept, runs on WebGPU when available, and adds a weather and season system, a 3D UI, and a daily leaderboard. It also has "Whispers": visitor messages screened with OpenAI's free moderation model ([case study](https://www.awwwards.com/brunos-portfolio-case-study.html)). Three ideas carry over directly. **Blender object names act as the level editor**: a name like `refLandingPhysicalFixed` becomes a fixed physics body, and Empties define bounds and respawn points. **A palette texture lets geometry merge** while each part keeps its own colour. **An automatic mobile preset** turns off water blur and depth of field and lowers shadow resolution. The case study does not mention screen readers or reduced motion, so this is not a model for accessibility.

**2. Giulio Collesei** is the closest conceptual sibling to One Night. It moves through four film-inspired scenes, including a neon-rain Blade Runner rooftop and a DeLorean finale. Its **rigged Blender android avatar of the owner** lifts its head when the visitor hovers over "About". The camera glides while the world dissolves, until only the avatar remains under a spotlight ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/04/14/they-call-me-giulio-the-making-of-a-cinematic-cyberpunk-portfolio/)). Several pipeline details are directly reusable. Animation actions are pushed to Blender's NLA so they export through glTF. Flying-car paths are Blender curves exported as point lists, a cheap way to author camera splines. gltf-transform caps textures at 1024 px before KTX2 conversion. Off-screen scenes pause rendering, and noise is baked into textures instead of computed in shaders.

**3. Joseph Santamaria** shows that a scroll site can feel like **"a single camera take"**. Scroll progress drives both camera movement and reveal timing. The lighting travels from an "early morning underwater city" through a red rose field into lava ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/04/28/more-than-a-portfolio-building-a-scroll-driven-3d-world-with-something-to-say/)). That palette journey is the nearest analogue found to dusk-to-dawn. Two techniques stand out. GSAP Observer gives wheel, touch and trackpad a single input path. Culling follows the known camera path, which replaces static LODs: if the camera path is fixed, you know what it will never see. Atmospheric passes render at low resolution on mobile. The site claims 144 fps on capable hardware and steady frame rates on mid-range phones. These are the author's own numbers.

**4. Igloo Inc** is the only verified Site of the Year in the set, and the benchmark for narrative transitions between scenes. Transitions combine chromatic aberration, "tech displacement" and frost. The intro is **rendered in real time rather than as video, to save size**. Textures load and shaders compile in the background during and after the first load, and quality drops on low-end devices ([case study](https://awwwards.com/igloo-inc-case-study.html)). It is not a portfolio and not open source. Its UI is drawn in WebGL, not HTML, which is the opposite of what PSYCH's accessibility bar requires.

**5. itomdev** is the best precedent for the Mind Palace look and for the ink-to-real moment. The whole site is flat planes carrying hand-drawn textures, with no 3D models. Its **`PaintRevealMaterial`** extends MeshBasicMaterial through `onBeforeCompile`, so a noise-masked progress value floods a sketch with paint on hover. This also tells the visitor what is clickable ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/06/11/sketching-the-impossible-a-3d-portfolio-built-without-a-single-3d-model/)). The site removed every light and baked shading into texture tints. It mounts only three 80-unit corridor segments at once, and pre-warms shaders with `compileAsync`. It also **dropped KTX2 because compression degraded line art and kept WebP**, a lesson that applies directly to ink textures. It tiers devices at load using `hardwareConcurrency`, `deviceMemory` and screen size, downgrades at runtime with drei's `PerformanceMonitor`, and supports arrow keys and PageUp/PageDown. Its awards are self-reported only.

**6. ProjectHub** is the only assistant in the research that meets all five marks of real engineering, as opposed to a gimmick. First, a regex intent classifier routes **more than 40 question types to deterministic handlers** over a JSON knowledge file, with no LLM call. Second, generated replies are **rejected if they add facts or numbers absent from the context**. Third, safety checks run before the model is called. Fourth, a router spreads load across Groq, Workers AI, GitHub Models, Gemini and others, with per-provider cooldowns and circuit breakers. Fifth, if every provider fails, the grounded answer is still returned ([dev.to](https://dev.to/bradleymatera/how-i-built-projecthub-an-embeddable-ai-recruiter-assistant-that-runs-on-free-tiers-bif)). It stores no conversations. One weakness: it gives no source citations, which is exactly where The Order's design goes further.

**7. Messenger** contributes the single best idea for changing the hour. **Every colour in the world comes from a 16×16 colour atlas**, so swapping or blending the atlas changes the whole mood at once ([case study](https://www.awwwards.com/messenger.html)). It also has a custom outline system with adjustable thickness and colour for a hand-drawn look. It uses a 4-level LOD that preserves silhouettes. It frees assets aggressively, because iPhone Safari kills pages that go over their memory limit.

**8. Lusion Oryzo** shows how a top studio uses Gaussian splats in production. Lusion tested real-time PBR and rejected it as below its quality bar. It then rendered the scene offline and trained splats from those renders. It uses **about 78,000 splats on desktop against 44,700 on mobile**, and only for props and reflections, because a 900,000-splat full-scene test still had flaws ([Lusion blog](https://blog.lusion.co/oryzo-bts-part-2-7-3d-design-and-motion-graphics)). It also placed training cameras on a sphere, not on the scroll path, which is useful advice for capturing the owner.

**9. Martin Laxenaire** is the template for the fast path and for quality tiers. The game can be skipped. Every WebGPU scene has a 2D-canvas fallback. An `FPSWatcher` feeds a `QualityManager`, which sets a 0–10 score against a default 55 fps target and reduces shadow samples, raymarch steps or post-processing as needed ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/10/06/self-doubt-and-the-quest-for-fun-how-i-ended-up-turning-my-portfolio-into-a-game/)). One WebGPU device drives four canvases, and rendering pauses on `visibilitychange`.

**10. Maggie Appleton's garden** sets the content pattern. Maturity is a Zod-validated frontmatter field (`growthStage`, with values seedling, budding or evergreen). Wiki-links are parsed by a remark plugin, and build scripts emit `links.json` and `internal-link-previews.json` for backlinks and hover previews ([content.config.ts](https://github.com/MaggieAppleton/maggieappleton.com-V3/blob/main/src/content.config.ts); [scripts](https://github.com/MaggieAppleton/maggieappleton.com-V3/tree/main/src/scripts)). It has no graph view. For the graph itself, **Quartz** (MIT, 13,619 stars) is the open-source reference: a d3-force layout drawn with PixiJS from a link index built at build time ([graph docs](https://github.com/jackyzha0/quartz/blob/v5/docs/features/graph%20view.md)). Gwern.net adds `status`, `confidence` and `importance` metadata plus hover popups ([gwern.net/about](https://gwern.net/about)).

Several technique sources fell just outside the ten but matter for specific features. **Maxime Heckel's Moebius post-process** is the canonical open-source ink shader ([blog](https://blog.maximeheckel.com/posts/moebius-style-post-processing)). **Eliseo Martelli's homepage** shows a person as a splat ([blog](https://www.eliseomartelli.it/blog/2026-03-28-gaussian-splat-portrait/)). **Cameron Rye's "Ask"** is a Cloudflare-native RAG bot ([blog](https://ryedev.pages.dev/blog/building-ask-rag-portfolio-chatbot)). **Ed Donner's Career Conversation** pattern adds a `record_unknown_question` tool ([HF Space](https://huggingface.co/spaces/grumppie/career_converstaion/blob/main/app.py)). **Shopify Editions Spring '26** uses a Tier 0–3 quality system ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/06/26/engineering-the-web-experience-behind-shopifys-spring-26-edition-everywhere/)). **Henry Heffernan's** MIT retro-computer site is widely forked, but no award for it could be verified ([GitHub](https://github.com/henryjeff/portfolio-website)).

Two cautions apply to this table. The first concerns lighting. Splats carry baked lighting, and One Night's lighting changes hour by hour. Each pose must therefore be graded to its own hour, not lit dynamically. That fits the design's frozen-moments approach, but it means the 21:00 bar pose and the dawn pose need separate grading. The second concerns ink-on-graph. A force-directed layout settles differently each time it runs. If the Palace must look hand-composed, pre-computing node positions at build time and storing them in `graph.json` would keep the drawing stable. That is an inference, not a sourced finding.

## Implications for later stack decisions (inputs, not decisions)

Everything in this section is an **input** to decisions still to be made. None of it is a final decision.

**Content backend and blog.** Original Contentlayer is abandoned: its last release was v0.3.4 in June 2023. Its fork Contentlayer2 has had no release since May 2025. **Content Collections** (active, MIT) and **Fumadocs MDX** (very active) are maintained, type-safe options. The lowest lock-in path is plain MDX + gray-matter + Zod in a build script that also emits `graph.json` ([maintenance table sources](https://github.com/sdorra/content-collections); [contentlayer](https://github.com/contentlayerdev/contentlayer)). For the post-launch admin, the most controllable option is a Next.js route behind Auth.js GitHub sign-in. It would allow exactly one user, matched by **numeric GitHub user ID**, not login name. It would commit through Octokit with a fine-grained token scoped to that one repo, and run the same Zod schema before committing. This last design is inferred; no external example was found. Keystatic (GitHub mode, MIT, pre-1.0) is the best off-the-shelf fallback. Neither it nor Sveltia has a per-user allowlist: any account with write access to the repo can log in ([Keystatic](https://keystatic.com/docs/github-mode); [Sveltia](https://sveltiacms.app/en/docs/backends/github)). Sveltia's PAT mode keeps the token in localStorage, which conflicts with the design's non-negotiable security stance.

**Database.** **None is needed at launch.** All content is static and validated at build time. For post-launch inquiries, **Cloudflare D1** (100,000 writes/day free) and **Turso** (10 million writes/month free) never pause ([D1](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/pricing/); [Turso](https://turso.tech/pricing)). Neon suspends compute after 5 minutes idle, which adds a cold start but costs nothing ([Neon](https://neon.com/pricing)). **Supabase pauses free projects after a week of inactivity**, a poor fit for a rarely used inquiry table ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/pricing)). Whether these free tiers allow commercial use was not checked. That matters for client inquiries, as does the design's own note that Vercel Hobby is non-commercial.

**AI assistant at $0.** **Cloudflare Workers AI** has the clearest no-card allowance: **10,000 Neurons/day, reset at 00:00 UTC**. Requests fail rather than bill once the cap is hit ([pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/pricing/)). That hard failure is exactly the "overspending impossible" property the design asks for. The researcher estimated about **500 answers a day** on llama-3.2-3b, assuming about 2k input and 300 output tokens per answer. That is a calculation, not a measurement. Small models of that size make code-side citation checks more important, not less. Groq is a plausible secondary provider: it returns HTTP 429 with `retry-after`, offers Zero Data Retention, and has a Prompt Guard model usable as an injection classifier. However, **its Free-plan limits and card requirement were not visible** on the public page ([Groq](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits)). Three options look weak. **Gemini's free tier** lets Google train on prompts and have humans review them, and **its terms bar serving users in the EEA, UK or Switzerland** ([terms](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/terms)). OpenRouter allows only 50 free requests/day with no purchase ([limits](https://openrouter.ai/docs/api/reference/limits)). Hugging Face gives free users no inference credits ([pricing](https://huggingface.co/docs/inference-providers/pricing)). For a corpus of tens to hundreds of posts, no vector database is needed. Precomputed embeddings in a static file of roughly 0.2–1.5 MB (a calculation), or BM25 over a JSON index, would do. There is also a hosting tension. The Cloudflare-native stack co-locates model, rate-limit store and logs. The design names Vercel. The research did not examine calling Workers AI from a Vercel route. Workers AI's data-use policy was also not checked, and the design requires data retention to be checked before a provider is chosen.

**3D rendering stack.** Every cited award winner (Igloo, Messenger) shipped on **WebGL three.js**. The newest portfolios (Bruno Simon, Giulio) write TSL for WebGPURenderer, which falls back to WebGL2 automatically ([three.js manual](https://threejs.org/manual/en/webgpurenderer)). WebGPU now ships in Safari 26. However, **iOS 26.0–26.2 had a WebGPU bug serious enough that the Construct engine disabled it**. A fix appeared in the iOS 26.4 beta; whether it reached the shipping release is unconfirmed ([WebKit 301627](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=301627)). The splat choice pulls the same way. **Spark is WebGL2-only**, while three.js r186's native splats require WebGPURenderer and have no LoD ([Spark 2.0](https://worldlabs.ai/blog/spark-2.0); [Houston](https://ben3d.ca/blog/how-to-use-threejs-native-gaussian-splats)). Taken together, the lower-risk default is R3F + WebGL + pmndrs postprocessing + Spark. Heckel's ink shader is GLSL, so it fits the same path. WebGPU/TSL is worth choosing only for specific gains, such as compute particles for The Order's liquid pour or MRT buffers for a cheaper outline pass, and with a pinned three.js version. GSAP ScrollTrigger + Observer + Lenis is the de facto choreography layer; **none of the reviewed sites used Theatre.js**. Recommended asset formats are glTF + Draco + KTX2, but WebP for line-art textures.

**Capture of the owner's likeness.** **Classic Scaniverse on iOS gives free, unlimited on-device splats.** The New Scaniverse Free plan exports PLY/SPZ with 20,000 credits/month. Free users may use assets "for their own projects", but commercial rights require Pro ([Niantic pricing](https://nianticspatial.com/pricing)). Check that clause before the site carries client inquiries. Polycam's free splat export is unverified. KIRI's splat capture is Pro-only, Postshot's free tier reportedly cannot export, and Luma has wound down. The pipeline would be: capture in flat light, crop and grade in SuperSplat 3.0, decimate to about 300K splats, then export SOG for Spark. The size estimate of about 5–15 MB is **an extrapolation from compression ratios, not a measurement** ([Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/gaussian-splatting-guide)). On privacy, research shows Gaussian-splat faces enable face swap and reenactment ([arXiv 2509.11624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11624v1)). Practical mitigations line up with the design: an amber grade with reduced spherical harmonics, camera framing that avoids a clean frontal face, and shipping only compressed files. Any client-rendered file can be downloaded, so these measures reduce risk rather than remove it. Capture guides also warn that people are harder subjects than still objects ([Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/how-to-capture-radiance-fields)). That favours the seated, supported poses already planned.

## What remains unverified and unfound

Several claims in this report are open. **No site was found** with a time-of-day journey, a first/third-person camera switch, a sketch-to-photoreal transition with a write-up, a human splat on an award-recognised portfolio, or a reveal of its own attention design. **Award gaps:** no Awwwards award could be verified for folio-2025, Giulio, Santamaria, Laxenaire, Henry Heffernan, Jesse Zhou, Messenger or Oryzo. itomdev's and Lusion's awards are self-reported. **Performance gaps:** there are **no measured Spark frame rates on a named mid-range phone**, and no 2026 mobile WebGPU benchmarks. Spark's depth behaviour under DoF and SSAO is also undocumented, and the mobile splat budgets cited are vendor targets. **Provider gaps:** the Groq Free-plan limits, Gemini Flash free limits, Workers AI data-use terms, OpenRouter free-model logging and GitHub Models limits are all unconfirmed. **Source gaps:** the full Codrops write-ups for Shader.se and Shopify could not be fetched (HTTP 403), and Lusion's tricks posts were not read. ProjectHub's page shows no year. Its repo licence and Toukoum's grounding method were not checked. Hosting stacks were not stated for most sites, and no one checked that every site is still live.

## Conclusion

The research changes the risk profile of PSYCH more than its direction. The hardest-sounding features turn out to be assemblies of documented parts: the ink world, the persistent camera, the grounded bartender and the realistic likeness. The truly novel parts are the cheap-sounding ones nobody has shipped: an hour-by-hour lighting blend, a clock that scrubs a camera path, and a camera that moves from seeing the owner to seeing through his eyes. These deserve early prototypes on a real mid-range phone, because no reference can say whether they will hold 30 fps. The second takeaway is that PSYCH's non-negotiables set it apart from the field. The reviewed award winners score weakly on accessibility, and none documents reduced-motion support. Most AI portfolio bots have no server-side limits, and none validates citations in code. If PSYCH clears its own quality bar, the fast path, the citation checks and the security posture will be differentiators a technical reviewer notices, as much as the 3D.
